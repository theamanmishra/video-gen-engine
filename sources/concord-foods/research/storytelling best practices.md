# Storytelling best practices for the demo film

What proven demo, launch and explainer makers do, and what each lesson means for the Concord film. Used to shape the frame table in [film brief](<../1 story/film brief.md>). Competitor demos are covered in [competitor playbook](<competitor playbook.md>), not here.

**How this was gathered (2026-11-14).** Desk research only. No video was watched in this session; every example below was read about (articles, transcripts, summaries). Most sources are vendor or agency blogs with a commercial interest; treat their numbers as directional.

## 1. Hook: the first 5 to 10 seconds

| Lesson | Source | What it means for us |
| --- | --- | --- |
| The opening is where most viewers leave. MrBeast's internal guide calls the first minute "the most important minute" and puts the most effort there | Leaked "How to Succeed at MrBeast Production" guide, summarised by [Alexander Jarvis](https://www.alexanderjarvis.com/memo-how-to-succeed-in-mrbeast-production/) and [Net Influencer](https://www.netinfluencer.com/mrbeast-productions-leaked-internal-document-rosanna-pansino/). Authenticity disputed | Spend the most care on 0:00 to 0:40 |
| Open with a concrete incident or the viewer's pain, not with a logo or someone introducing themselves | [Spiel Creative](https://www.spielcreative.com/blog/?p=18285), [Yans Media](https://www.yansmedia.com/blog/explainer-video-hook-techniques), [Advids](https://advids.co/insights/the-importance-of-the-hook-crafting-the-first-5-seconds-of-your-b2b-video) (agency blogs) | The tipping jug is the right first image. Founders must not open the film |
| The physical incident as hook is already proven in our category. Cerrion's 12.68 s fallen-container film puts the fallen can on screen first, then highlights it near 6.6 s | [competitor playbook](<competitor playbook.md>), Cerrion section (read, not watched here) | Get to the tip by about 0:04. Add a timestamp so it feels like a real moment |
| Name the viewer early | [Advids](https://advids.co/insights/the-importance-of-the-hook-crafting-the-first-5-seconds-of-your-b2b-video) | Say "ILFA-2" on screen in the first seconds, and the founders address Andrea and Mike by name |
| A hook needs a turn, not just an event. Story beats should connect with "but" or "therefore", never "and then" | Trey Parker and Matt Stone, NYU class 2011, via [No Film School](https://nofilmschool.com/2015/04/tony-zhou-storytelling-video-essay-f-fake-therefore-but-and-then) | "The jug tipped and then someone fixed it" is flat. "It was fixed in 54 seconds. **But** it was the fourth tip today, and nobody logged why" is a hook |

## 2. Founders on camera

| Lesson | Source | What it means for us |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A founder or maker on camera makes a launch video feel personal. Sandwich (Adam Lisagor) made hundreds of 1.5 to 2 minute startup videos and often put a real person on camera selling the product | [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adam_Lisagor), [Wistia interview](https://wistia.com/blog/sandwich-video-interview), [Entrepreneur](https://www.entrepreneur.com/article/243507) | Andrea and Mike have met the founders. Their faces add trust no animation can |
| A talking head is a weak *opening*; the strong opening shows the product or the problem | [Spiel Creative](https://www.spielcreative.com/blog/?p=18285) | Founders come in after the cold open, not before |
| Talk to the camera from an outline, not a script read aloud. All founders appear. Audio quality matters more than picture | YC application-video advice via [Startup Grind](https://www.startupgrind.com/blog/y-combinator-application-video-tutorial) and [Flonnect](https://flonnect.com/blog/?p=2510) | Phone camera is fine. Quiet room, phone close to the mouth or a cheap wired mic |
| Face plus screen ("webcam bubble over a screen share") is reported as the best-responding sales-video format | [Vidyard](https://www.vidyard.com/product/personalized-video) (vendor survey, no method given) | Founders can appear in a corner over the twin at the end, not only full screen |

## 3. Structure: problem, agitate, solve, before and after

| Lesson | Source | What it means for us |
| --- | --- | --- |
| The classic explainer is a person with a relatable problem, a simple solution and plain benefits. Dropbox's 2009 homepage video (Common Craft, about 2:17) is the usual reference | [Idea Rocket](https://idearocketanimation.com/6164-can-learn-dropboxs-famous-explainer-video/), [Yans Media case study](https://www.yansmedia.com/blog/dropboxs-mvp-explainer-video-case-study). Claims of its conversion impact are [UNVERIFIED] | One problem, felt by a person, solved in front of them |
| Agitate means making the cost of the problem visible, not repeating it | [UNVERIFIED] general copywriting practice | Show the stops piling up across a shift. That is the agitation |
| Bookend: return to the opening moment, now with the product | [UNVERIFIED] common film practice; matches Aman's handwritten page 3, point 5 ("before and after") | Close on the same jug leaning again. This time it is caught early |

## 4. Demo pacing

| Lesson | Source | What it means for us |
| --- | --- | --- |
| "Do the last thing first": open with the end result, then drill into detail | Peter Cohan, *Great Demo!*, via [Pragmatic Institute](https://pragmaticinstitute.com/resources/webinars/product/create-and-deliver-surprisingly-compelling-software-demonstrations) | After the founders, show the whole twin and the answer ("the filler lost the most time") before the deep dive |
| Gong's analysis of 67,149 sales demos: winners use an "upside down pyramid", best part first. Saving the best for last is "the single most common sales demo mistake". The opening context runs under two minutes | [Gong](https://www.gong.io/blog/sales-demos) (vendor research, correlational) | Do not hold the twin back. Architecture goes late and short |
| Show, don't tell | YC, quoted by [Pilot](https://pilot.com/blog/how-prepare-yc-application-video) | Every claim is an action on screen: a click, a panel, an answer |

## 5. Length

| Lesson | Source | What it means for us |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Shorter videos keep a larger share of viewers. Under one minute averages 52% engagement on Wistia (13 million videos) | [Wistia, 2025](https://wistia.com/blog/optimal-video-length) | Length must be earned scene by scene |
| Right length depends on intent; Wistia warns against one magic number. Sandwich launch films sit at 1.5 to 2 minutes | [Wistia](https://wistia.com/blog/optimal-video-length), [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adam_Lisagor) | This is a personal send to two people who already know the plant and asked for this. About 2:30 is fine. Over 3:00 starts to cost attention [UNVERIFIED] judgement |

## 6. The ask that tests willingness to pay

| Lesson | Source | What it means for us |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Praise is not data. A real signal costs the customer time, reputation or money. A meeting without a commitment "didn't happen" | Rob Fitzpatrick, *The Mom Test*, summarised by [mtlynch](https://mtlynch.io/book-reports/the-mom-test/) | End with one concrete step that costs them something, not "let us know what you think" |
| Have the willingness-to-pay conversation early, with a prototype or concept, before building | Ramanujam and Tacke, *Monetizing Innovation*, via [Graham Mann notes](https://grahammann.net/book-notes/monetizing-innovation-georg-tacke-madhavan-ramanujam) | The film is the concept. The ask is a paid pilot on ILFA-2 |
| End on one clear next step | [Spiel Creative](https://www.spielcreative.com/blog/?p=18285), [Flowjam](https://www.flowjam.com/blog/startup-product-demo-video) (agency blogs) | Founders on camera make the ask in their own words. Price goes in the covering email, not in the film, because the film may be forwarded inside Sugar Foods and the price may change [UNVERIFIED] judgement |

## 7. Our own Nashuba Bakery film

Aman's earlier client film, `../../../nashuba bakery/Shiftwork-for-John.mp4` (4:50). Reviewed as stills every 3 seconds on 2026-11-14; motion inferred from consecutive stills, audio not reviewed. Patterns adopted in the Concord storyboard:

| Pattern | What it looks like there | Used in Concord |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Illustrated founders | Cartoon Aman and Adarsh with name tags open, introduce the problems and return for the ask | Frames 2, 5, 12 |
| Agenda reveal | "Three problems stuck with us": numbered items appear one by one with icons, key words underlined | Frame 2 |
| Eyebrow label | Small caps "ONE · COST PER LOAF" above every title | P1 / P2 / HOW IT WORKS / RESULTS / NEXT STEP |
| Action titles | One-sentence serif takeaway, built word by word | Every screen from Frame 2 |
| Missing-piece pills | Formula pills show "?" when unknown and flip to ✓ ("Video fills in the missing pieces") | The stop-record card linking P1/P2 to the solution |
| One big callout | "5.6× longer, same bench, same packer" beside the chart | One callout per data screen |
| Ruled out vs our pick | X-ray card stamped "RULED OUT", camera card ribboned "OUR PICK" | Frame 10a |
| Camera inset | "What the camera sees" panel with a verdict tag beside a numbered process strip | Frames 6, 8 |
| Pilot close | "How a pilot would work" in three steps, then "Can we run a pilot on...?" and "Thank you, John." | Frame 12, without the company name the Nashuba end card showed |
| Small honesty footnotes | "Bar lengths are illustrative, not measured" on each data screen | Open question for the film session: the approved Concord look allows one "Illustrative" tag and no per-frame small print |
