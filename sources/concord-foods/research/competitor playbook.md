# Competitor demo playbook

Private working reference for Shiftwork's Sugar Foods / Concord Foods ILFA-2 product-demo video. Compiled on 2026-11-12 from the sixteen original company reports, subsequent source checks and the recorded manufacturer conversations.

## Start here

This file answers one question. Which product and marketing elements should the video consider, why are they persuasive and how would they serve this customer?

Use the customer lens and scene map first, then read the relevant company sections. The asset table provides a short browsing route through all sixteen competitors. Every company has the same framework, with product design and marketing design separated. Each reusable element has an ID so a storyboard, script or generation brief can cite its source idea.

Aman said the Cerrion and Augmentir examples seem right and delegated the remaining extraction. Continue the analysis and propose a coherent film without requiring him to review or approve each competitor. The ideas are recommendations for a proposed product experience. They are not evidence that Shiftwork already ships these capabilities or that a final script, price or production tool is approved.

The [customer and plant context](<customer and plant context.md>) remains the source for the brief, plant facts, exact customer requirements, data limits and detailed research qualifications. The original consolidated research reports were removed on 2026-11-14 to cut duplication; this playbook and its source links are now the single competitor record. No originating chat, agent memory or particular harness is needed to use this playbook. Raw plant footage and a faithful geometry model are not bundled.

## The review framework

Work backwards from what Andrea, Mike or an operator must recognize, understand and believe before the product feels worth buying.

| Lens | What to extract | Required video consequence |
|---|---|---|
| Proposition | Recognizable problem, target user, operating payoff and distinctive promise | State the customer belief this part of the film should earn |
| Product design | Capabilities, workflow, interaction, information hierarchy and worker result | Specify the exact product action and visible response |
| Marketing design | Art direction, typography, composition, imagery, graphics, motion, transitions, pacing and sound where inspected | Specify how the scene makes that action legible and memorable |
| Communication | Opening, sequence, language, reveal, payoff and call to action | Explain how this moment advances the story rather than add an isolated feature |
| Tech and credibility | Mechanism, inputs, rules, models, expertise, review, deployment, customer proof and uncertainty | State the evidence or implementation condition needed to support the promise |

These are the user's four lenses, with Design shown as two rows. Record the vendor observation separately from our proposed adaptation. Static images establish composition and content. They do not establish motion, soundtrack or live application behaviour. Proposed camera moves and animation below are our creative recommendations unless specifically described as observed footage.

Each element records its source and moment, the persuasive pattern, proposed product and film treatment, intended viewer belief, customer fit and proof requirement. Several sources may inform one scene. The synthesis recommends a small coherent set rather than placing every element into the film.
